## 🪞 Reflection: Where I Stand at the End of Month 1

By the end of Week 4, I've started to get a clearer picture of where I actually stand in the industry. After spending this first month learning surface-level foundations, I've discovered that I genuinely enjoy a lot of what Unreal Engine has to offer — that part is no longer in question.

What *is* in question is the gap between my current background and the skill level the roadmap (and the Technical Artist role itself) eventually expects. For a while, spending too much time watching professionals in the industry led to a lot of overthinking, especially since I'm only one month into this. But every time I went back into Unreal and actually built something, that overthinking dropped. That's a clear signal: **building cuts through overthinking, comparing fuels it.** Going forward, the focus shifts from comparing myself to others toward consistently building things.

---

## 🔍 The Gap I've Identified

There's a real gap between what I've been doing (foundation-focused, tutorial-driven learning) and what the industry realistically expects from someone working toward a Technical Artist role. Recognizing this gap isn't a setback — it's useful information that changes my next move.

Becoming a Technical Artist directly, right now, isn't a realistic short-term goal — and that's fine. Instead of treating that as failure, I'm adjusting the plan:

- I'll **keep following the existing roadmap** and continue uploading progress to this repo (Technical-Artist-Journey), with some edits to the `README.md` to better reflect this adjusted direction.
- I'll **start a separate repository** dedicated to actually building a game in Unreal Engine — to get hands-on with the *full, realistic process* of game development, not just isolated technical exercises.

This isn't abandoning the Technical Artist goal — it's closing the gap between theory/foundations and the practical reality of a full production pipeline.

---

## 🎯 Possible Entry Points Going Forward

Rather than fixating on "Technical Artist" as the only acceptable entry role, I'm opening up to realistic adjacent roles that build directly on the same skill set:

- Unreal Developer
- Technical Developer
- Simulation Developer
- XR Developer
- Interactive Developer
- Junior Technical Artist

All of these share overlapping foundations with the TA roadmap, so none of the work done so far is wasted — it's just being aimed at a wider, more realistic set of entry points.
