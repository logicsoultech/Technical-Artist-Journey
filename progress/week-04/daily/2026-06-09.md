## 📖 Theory: What is PBR and Why Does It Matter?

**PBR (Physically Based Rendering)** is a shading and rendering methodology that accurately represents how light interacts with surfaces using mathematical formulas derived from real-world physics — not artistic approximation or "whatever looks good."

Before PBR, artists had to craft materials that looked correct under one specific lighting condition. If the scene's lighting changed, the material could immediately look wrong. PBR solves this through the principle of **energy conservation** — a surface cannot reflect more light than it receives.

**Why it matters for a Technical Artist:**
- Industry standard across all modern engines (UE5, Unity HDRP, Godot 4)
- TAs are responsible for ensuring the art team's material pipeline adheres to correct PBR standards
- Understanding PBR enables TAs to systematically troubleshoot why a material "looks wrong"
- Foundation for all shading work, VFX materials, and texture optimization covered in later phases

## 📦 What is AmbientCG?

[AmbientCG](https://ambientcg.com) is the largest free PBR texture library available, released under a **CC0 Public Domain** license — free for personal and commercial use with no attribution required.

It offers over 2,000 PBR materials, HDRIs, and 3D models at resolutions up to 8K. Every material ships with all the texture maps needed for a PBR workflow: Color, Normal, Roughness, AO, Displacement, and sometimes Metallic.

**Why it's great for learning:**
- All textures are seamless (tileable) — ready to use without any editing
- NormalGL (OpenGL) format, directly compatible with UE5
- Filterable by material category, resolution, and source (photographic vs. procedural)
- Real-world reference for how physical materials should actually look

## 🗺️ Concept Map: The 6 Texture Maps in a PBR System

Each PBR material is composed of several texture maps, each storing different information about a surface.

### 1. Albedo / Base Color
- The pure surface color with no lighting information, highlights, or shadows baked in
- This is the material's "true color" under neutral light
- **Do not** include baked shadows or AO in this map
- For metals: albedo defines the reflection color (gold → gold-tinted reflections)
- For non-metals: albedo = diffuse color
- **Color space: sRGB** (the only map that uses sRGB)

### 2. Normal Map
- Stores surface normal vector directions encoded as RGB values (X=R, Y=G, Z=B)
- Creates the illusion of geometric detail — bumps, ridges, cracks — without adding polygons
- The base color of a normal map is blue-purple (`#8080FF`), meaning the surface is flat and facing straight up
- **Does not alter the 3D silhouette** — it's purely a lighting illusion
- Two formats: **NormalGL** (OpenGL — for UE5/Blender) and **NormalDX** (DirectX — for some older engines)
- **Color space: Linear / Non-Color, sRGB OFF**

### 3. Roughness
- Grayscale map controlling how microscopically rough or smooth a surface is
- **White (1.0)** = very rough → diffuse, wide reflections
- **Black (0.0)** = very smooth/glossy → sharp, bright reflections
- Variation in roughness is key to realism — a flat, uniform value always looks fake
- ⚠️ In Unity HDRP/URP this value is **inverted** and called Smoothness (white = smooth)
- **Color space: Linear, sRGB OFF**

### 4. Metallic
- Essentially a binary map: **white (1.0)** = metal, **black (0.0)** = non-metal
- Gray values should only appear at transition edges between metal and non-metal (a few pixels at most)
- Metals reflect light tinted by their albedo color; non-metals reflect neutral/white specular
- The vast majority of real-world materials are non-metal → Metallic = 0 (Constant node)
- **Color space: Linear, sRGB OFF**

### 5. Ambient Occlusion (AO)
- Grayscale map estimating how much ambient light can reach a given point on the surface
- Dark in crevices, hidden corners, and tight gaps; white = fully exposed to light
- Adds visual weight, depth, and grounding to a material
- **Do not** bake AO directly into Albedo — use the dedicated AO slot in the engine
- For advanced optimization: pack into the R channel of an ORM texture
- **Color space: Linear, sRGB OFF**

### 6. Displacement / Height Map
- Grayscale map that **physically displaces 3D geometry vertices**
- White = pushed outward, black = pushed inward
- Produces real 3D silhouette changes, unlike Normal maps which are purely illusory
- Very expensive — use only with tightly controlled tessellation budgets
- **Not supported with UE5 Nanite** — use Parallax Occlusion Mapping (POM) as an alternative
- **Color space: Linear, sRGB OFF**

## ✅ What I Did Today

### 1. Downloading Textures from AmbientCG
- Downloaded **Bricks071** at 2K resolution, PNG format from [ambientcg.com](https://ambientcg.com)
- Identified each texture map file from the extracted ZIP:
  - `_Color.png` → Albedo / Base Color
  - `_NormalGL.png` → Normal Map (OpenGL format, directly compatible with UE5)
  - `_Roughness.png` → Roughness
  - `_AmbientOcclusion.png` → Ambient Occlusion
  - `_Displacement.png` → Height / Displacement
  - *(No Metallic map — non-metal material)*

### 2. Import & Texture Settings in UE5.4
- Drag-dropped all PNGs into the UE5.4 Content Browser
- Set correct compression settings per texture (double-click → Details panel):

| Texture | Compression | sRGB |
|---|---|---|
| Color / Albedo | Default (DXT1/5) | ✅ ON |
| Normal Map | Normalmap (BC5) | ❌ OFF |
| Roughness | Masks | ❌ OFF |
| AO | Masks | ❌ OFF |
| Displacement | Displacementmap | ❌ OFF |

- Understood why sRGB must be disabled for data maps — they carry linear mathematical data, not perceptual color information.

### 3. Building the Material Graph `M_Brickslearn1`
- Created a new Material from the Content Browser
- Used **`MF_Tiling`** as the UV input for all TextureSample nodes — centralizes tiling/scale control
- Handled the absence of a Metallic map using a **Constant node set to 0.0** → Metallic pin
- Connected Roughness and AO using the **R output pin** (not RGB) since they are grayscale
- Connected Normal map directly via RGB → Normal pin (valid in UE5, no additional NormalMap node required)

### 4. Non-Destructive Modification: ColorTint
- Added a **VectorParameter** named `ColorTint` (default white: `1,1,1`)
- Multiplied ColorTint × Color texture output → Base Color
- Shifted the brick color toward red without touching the original texture
- This is **non-destructive workflow** — the source texture is never modified

### 5. Creating Material Instances
- Right-clicked `M_Brickslearn1` → Create Material Instance
- Created multiple instances (`MI_Brickslearn1`, `MI_Brickslearn2`)
- ColorTint can be adjusted per instance without entering the material graph

## 📝 Technical Artist Notes for UE5.4

**Nanite & Displacement**
Nanite does not natively support displacement maps. For surface depth on Nanite meshes, use **Parallax Occlusion Mapping (POM)** in the material graph as a substitute. POM simulates surface depth without altering actual geometry.

**Lumen & AO**
Because Lumen calculates indirect lighting dynamically, AO textures have a subtler influence compared to engines with baked lighting. AO textures remain useful primarily for fine details in crevices that Lumen's calculations do not fully resolve.

**Texture Tiling & Address Mode**
AmbientCG textures are seamless. The Address Mode must be set to **Wrap** (not Clamp) for correct tiling. Visible seam lines are almost always caused by an incorrect Address Mode setting.

**Color Space Bug — Quick Diagnosis**
If a material looks overly metallic, too glossy, or has inconsistent roughness across different lighting conditions → in 90% of cases the cause is sRGB left enabled on the Roughness, Metallic, or Normal map.

**ORM Packing (Advanced Optimization)**
Pack AO (R) + Roughness (G) + Metallic (B) into a single texture. Saves two texture samples per material — significant for performance in scenes with many distinct materials.

**Texture Resolution Budget**
- Background / far distance: 512 – 1K
- General environment: 2K
- Hero assets (close-up, primary objects): 4K
- Defining resolution budgets is one of the TA's responsibilities within the production pipeline.

## 💡 Insights & New Understanding

- PBR is not about "looking good" — it's about **physical consistency**. A correctly built material will look right under any lighting condition.
- Understanding *why* each map exists matters far more than simply knowing how to connect the nodes.
- Non-destructive workflow (parameters + instances) is a fundamental distinction between how a TA works versus a general artist.
- Clarification on NormalMap node: connecting RGB directly to the Normal pin is correct in UE5 as long as compression settings are properly configured.
